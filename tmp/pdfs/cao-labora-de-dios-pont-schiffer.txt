                                                                COUNTEREXAMPLES TO SCHIFFER’S CONJECTURE

                                                                         GONZALO CAO-LABORA AND JAUME DE DIOS PONT


                                                  A BSTRACT. The Schiffer conjecture states that if a smooth domain Ω ⊂ Rn admits a Neumann eigen-
                                                  function of the Laplacian which is constant at the boundary, then the domain is a ball. It is intimately
                                                  related to Pompeiu’s problem, stating that if a nonzero function integrates zero over any rigid motion of Ω,
                                                  then Ω is the ball.
                                                      We disprove both conjectures in R2 , constructing infinitely many planar domains Ω which are not balls
                                                  and satisfy the conditions above. Our domains are N -fold symmetric, with N sufficiently large. Our
                                                  approach is based on a novel strategy of considering a relaxed problem where N can be any real number
                                                  (which corresponds to Schiffer only if N is natural). Then, we apply bifurcation theory to such relaxed
                                                  problem, showing that the size of the local bifurcation branch can be taken independent of N . This result
                                                  allows us to conclude that branches that start with N sufficiently close to an integer reach integer values of
                                                  N.

connected boundary [3, 4], or if the third, fourth, or fifth interior normal derivative of u is constant on the
connected boundary [28, 24], then Ω must be a Euclidean ball. More recent rigidity results include the
case in which the spectral parameter equals the second Dirichlet eigenvalue [12], and certain classes of
centrally symmetric analytic planar domains [29]; see also the recent preprint [13] for a large-eigenvalue
result under central-symmetry and convexity assumptions.
   In the case n = 2, the Schiffer conjecture is also known to hold, for simply connected Ω, if λ is
among the seven lowest Neumann eigenvalues [2, 14], or if a solution u of (1.1) has no saddle points
[40].
   Regularity results are also available: importantly, Kinderlehrer and Nirenberg showed, under the hy-
potheses of their free-boundary regularity theorem [26], that both u and ∂Ω are real analytic (in particu-
lar, C ∞ ). In the planar Pompeiu setting, Williams [39] proved that a bounded Lipschitz counterexample
with connected boundary must have real-analytic boundary.
   In the direction of constructing counterexamples, bifurcating techniques were used by Wheeler [37],
in the case of a similar overdetermined Helmholtz problem. In the specific case of Schiffer, the foun-
dational work of Fall, Minlend and Weth [20] introduced the use of anisotropic spaces, which paved
the way towards constructing examples by bifurcation. Concretely, [20] disproved an analogue of the
Schiffer conjecture on the cylinder, as well as non-contractible counterexamples on the sphere (refuting
Souam’s conjecture [35]). After that, Enciso, Fernández, Ruiz and Sicbaldi [17] used a similar func-
tional setting to construct examples of non-contractible Ω in R2 with Neumann eigenfunctions that are
locally constant at the boundary (that is, ∂Ω has two connected components and u achieves different
constant values on each of them). The first author with Fernández [7] also uses a similar functional
setting to construct contractible Schiffer counterexamples on S2 .
   For broader accounts of Schiffer-type problems and of the classical literature on the Pompeiu problem,
we refer respectively to the surveys of Enciso [18] and Zalcman [42].
   We also remark that Schiffer-type and related overdetermined constructions have important conse-
quences for stationary Euler flows (see the note by Kelliher [25] and [21, 10, 15, 17, 23]).
   We point out that on August 3rd, Colbrook and Stepaniants posted on arXiv an independent con-
struction of a Schiffer counterexample [9]. The two proofs take different approaches. In [9], a concrete
counterexample is constructed with 10-fold symmetry. The strategy is to use a computer-assisted proof
that validates the existence of an exact counterexample close to a numerical one. In contrast, the ex-
amples presented here provide an infinite sequence of N -fold symmetric counterexamples, and do not
require computer assistance. The strategy is to use bifurcation theory for a relaxed problem that allows
N to take non-integer values.

1.1. Strategy of the proof. Recent works on Schiffer-type problems have employed bifurcation tech-
niques in order to construct counterexamples [20, 17, 7]. The idea is to construct a continuous family
of solutions, such as balls of radius R with (λ, u) being the corresponding Neumann eigenpair. Then, at
the linear level, one sees that a bifurcation of this problem can occur if and only if λ is also a Dirichlet
eigenvalue of the initial bifurcating domain.
   The direct application of that idea does not work for R2 , because distinct integer-order Bessel func-
tions have no common nontrivial zeros [33]. We will circumvent the issue by instead considering Bessel
functions of non-integer order. We would like to construct N −fold symmetric counterexamples on a
ball of radius N (see Figure 1). If those counterexamples exist, they will descend to a counterexample
on a cone of length N and diameter 2π. Using polar coordinates in the cone, these examples will be
solutions to the PDE
                                                            N2

                                                    ∂r
                              (∆ + λ)u = ∂r2 + u + 2 ∂ψ2 + λ u = 0
                                                     r      r
in the domain (r, ψ) ∈ [0, N ] × S1 , together with the appropriate regularity conditions at r = 0. The
case when N is an integer corresponds to Laplace eigenfunctions with N −fold symmetry, which give
rise to integer-order Bessel functions. Instead, we will consider the problem above when N (henceforth
called R) is a large real number.
   As R goes to infinity, this long cone converges, formally, to a half-cylinder, where Schiffer counterex-
amples are known to exist. When making the argument rigorous, it is convenient to avoid working with
an infinite domain, and to avoid the singularity at the origin of the cone.

   To do so, we focus on a collared neighborhood of the boundary of the cylinder, and abstract the interior
of the ball with a Dirichlet-to-Neumann map, which encodes the interior behavior of the cylinder (most
importantly, the boundedness at the origin).
   With such an approach we will write a formal problem G(R, v) = 0 that can be lifted to the original
problem when R = N , but is an artificial problem if R ∈        / N (while it could be lifted to a problem
in the cone, we will not make much use of the geometry of the cone). We will follow the anisotropic
space approach introduced by Fall, Minlend and Weth [20], and encode both the boundary ∂Ω and the
eigenfunction u in the data v using anisotropic spaces that avoid a loss of derivative when trying to
perform bifurcation analysis of the problem G.
   In order to find solutions of G(R, v) = 0 with R ∈ N, we first understand the linearization of G(R, 0).
This is analogous to the approach performed in [20, 17, 7]. This allows us to construct a local bifurcation
branch G(R(s), v(s)) = 0 with R(0) = R⋆ .
   The crucial difference is that a local perturbation is not enough for our purposes, since we will perturb
from R⋆ ∈  / N. Instead, we prove a uniform perturbation version of the Crandall–Rabinowitz theorem,
that allows us to construct the branch G(R(s), v(s)) = 0 with s ∈ [−s̄, s̄], with s̄ independent of the
bifurcation point.
   That requires us to prove several uniform analogues of the standard bifurcation hypotheses (such as
uniform bounds on the operator G, on its linearization, or the transversality of the bifurcation points).
We then show that R′ (0) = 0 (the branch is of pitchfork type), R′′ (0) is bounded above by a negative
constant, and |R′′′ (s)| is uniformly bounded along the branch.
   An equidistribution argument on Bessel zeros then shows that the bifurcation point R∗ can be taken as
close as we want (from above) to an integer. This will allow us to conclude the existence of bifurcation
branches that, starting close enough to an integer (at distance smaller than − 12 R′′ (0)s2 ), can reach that
integer value through the bifurcation. One numerical example of the method described above is plotted
in Figure 1.
   This extension of the problem to non-integer R is needed to overcome the known perturbative rigid-
ity results near the disk. Agranovsky [1] treats real-analytic solution-carrying families with an analytic
branch of Pompeiu spectral parameters; Kobayashi [27] assumes, in particular, uniformly bounded spec-
tral parameters along a family issuing from the disk; and Canuto [6] proves rigidity near a fixed radial
Neumann eigenvalue in an eigenvalue-dependent perturbation class. These results do not cover the
regime considered here. Indeed, after rescaling our radius-N domains to unit size, the corresponding
eigenvalue is of order N 2 , and the noninteger part of our relaxed branch is not a family of planar do-
mains.
